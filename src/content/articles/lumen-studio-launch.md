Lumen Studio opens today for everyone. It replaces the three separate tools most teams keep open all day — the notes app, the board, and the doc — with one canvas that never asks you to decide where a thought belongs before you have had it.

The idea is simple. Most work begins as something shapeless: a fragment, a sketch, a half-sentence in a meeting. Software usually forces that fragment into a container immediately, and the container quietly dictates the rest of the process. Studio starts with no container at all.

> We stopped designing a document editor and started designing a surface that can become anything.

## A canvas that reads your intent

Every block in Studio is the same primitive. A paragraph can become a checklist, a checklist can become a board card, and a board card can become a section of a longer document — without copying, converting, or losing context.

- **One primitive.** Text, tasks, tables, and cards share a single model.
- **Local-first.** Edits land on disk before they reach the network.
- **Quiet by default.** No notification badges, no streaks, no scores.

## What ships today

Studio is available on macOS, Windows, iOS, and the web. Existing workspaces can be imported from markdown, Notion, and plain folders of files. Nothing is deleted during import, and everything stays exportable as markdown at any time.

For teams, shared canvases inherit permissions from the workspace, and every block keeps a full revision history that can be restored individually. We think the ability to undo a single paragraph six months later is table stakes, not a premium feature.

## What comes next

Over the next two quarters we are working on offline multiplayer, a public API for block-level automation, and a review mode designed for editorial teams. All three are in early access with design partners today.
