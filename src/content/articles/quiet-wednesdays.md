A year ago we removed every recurring meeting from Wednesday. Not moved — removed. If something needed to happen on a Wednesday, it had to be scheduled deliberately, by a person, for a reason.

## The first month was uncomfortable

People did not know what to do with the space. Several teams immediately filled it with ad-hoc calls, which we had explicitly not forbidden. By week three, the calendar was quietly as full as before.

So we added one rule: a Wednesday meeting requires a written note explaining why it cannot happen on another day. The note is public. That was enough. Scheduling dropped by 71% and never recovered.

## What people actually did

We surveyed the team in month six and again at twelve. The most common answers were unsurprising and slightly humbling:

- Deep work that had been postponed for months
- Reading documentation, specs, and each other's proposals
- Mentoring, usually informally and usually not scheduled
- Finishing things

The fourth one matters most. A surprising amount of the value came from people completing work they already understood, which had simply been interrupted every forty-five minutes.

## The cost

Wednesdays are now the worst day to reach anyone urgently. We accepted that trade explicitly, and built a rotation so that one person per team is always available for genuine emergencies.

We also lost some cross-team serendipity. The hallway conversation that used to happen between two meetings now needs to be scheduled on a Thursday, and sometimes it simply does not happen. That is a real cost, and we think it is smaller than the benefit — but it is not zero, and we do not pretend otherwise.

## Would we do it again

Yes, with one change: we would have started with the written-note rule instead of adding it in month three.
